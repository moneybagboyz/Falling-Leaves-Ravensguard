# Phase 5 fun check: property, building, crafting, businesses, vehicles and heirs

A 25 to 30 minute session (plan §6, "fun checks"). Play a normal new game (not Ironman for the first run, so F5 and F9 work). Building, crafting and waiting on a business take game days: use 8x (key 4) when nothing hostile is near, and F5 before each item so you can go back. After each item write one line under it: what happened, whether it was fun, and the answer to **"did you understand why that happened?"**. A "no" is the most useful answer: say what you expected instead.

Before you start: note the date, the build (title screen, bottom left), the PC, and whether you played Phase 4's check. F8 opens the bug report at any time (it adds a screenshot; nothing is sent). L opens the Ledger at any time: look at it after items 4, 5, 6 and 9.

The review behind this check is `docs/reviews/phase-5.md`; its known limits say what is not in yet (no treatment screen, no sowing a claim, the designer opens anywhere).

## 1. A cottage by hand (4 minutes)
Stand on open ground you may build on: a plot you bought (item 4 shows how), or stake a claim on wild land (Q > Stake a claim here). Press **B** for build mode.
- Place a few wall pieces with the left mouse (Shift and drag for an outline), turn with Z and X, a door, then R to roof over. Place a hearth and two pallets inside: a home needs them.
- Leave build mode (B), carry the materials to the pile (the site panel, J in build mode, says what is missing), stand by a ghost and work it. Speed up to 8x.
- Look for: ghosts turning into pieces one at a time, the materials leaving the pile as each piece starts, work stopping at night and in heavy rain, the pile losing a little thatch or wood if it sits for days.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Why did a piece wait (materials, support, rain, dark)? Did the site panel tell you?

## 2. Hire a crew (3 minutes)
On the same site, open the site panel (J in build mode) and hire two labourers (and a skilled hand, if the place has one).
- Watch a game day at 8x. Then empty your purse below a day's wages (spend or drop coin) and watch the next morning.
- Look for: the hands drawn at their pieces, the day's wages leaving your purse in the morning and the unworked part coming back in the evening, hands quitting after three unpaid days, the chronicle line when they do.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Was the pace what you expected for two hands? Why did they stop or quit?

## 3. Commission a builder (3 minutes)
Place a bigger plan you will not build yourself (P in build mode: the plan library), leave build mode, and open Q > Building work and builders. Compare the bids (materials, labour, haul, days) and sign one.
- Leave for a few game days (8x), then come back.
- Look for: the escrow leaving your purse at signing, the builder's cart bringing materials, his crew on the site, the money released as the work goes up, the handover.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Why did one bid cost more or take longer than another? Did the days match the bid?

## 4. Buy a lot and place a library plan (3 minutes)
Beside the reeve, mayor or steward of a town, Q > the lot market. Buy a plot (about 450 crowns; a dev purse is fine for this check) and look at it in the deeds list.
- In build mode on the plot, press P and pick a two-storey plan (the smithy house). Let it fit itself to the street, mirror it with M if it suits, Enter to commit.
- Look for: the plan snapping to the plot and its setbacks, the plan refusing a spot it does not fit (and saying why), both storeys of ghosts (PgUp and PgDn), the cost and hours in the library's preview.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Why did it fit or not fit where you put it?

## 5. Open a smithy and leave a steward (4 minutes)
Once the smithy house stands (finish item 4 with a builder, or use an existing workshop you buy), stand in its workshop and Q > Open a smithy here. Hire a smith, put iron bars in its store, hire a steward and give him orders (nails, send profit every few days). Then travel to another town for a game season (8x, or a long journey).
- Come back and open the Ledger (L): the Businesses tab's season rows, the home chest.
- Look for: revenue, wages, inputs and profit per season; the profit in the home chest, not your purse; what the steward did while you were away (bought, sold, hired).
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Did it make or lose money, and could you tell why from the Ledger?

## 6. Prospect and open a quarry (3 minutes)
Out in the hills, Q > Prospecting: walk the land and read it, pan a stream, dig a test pit. Write the report. Petition for a licence where you need one, then found a quarry or a pit on what you found (Q > Open a business here) and hire a few hands.
- Look for: what a walk tells you against what a pit or a shaft does, the report as an item in your pack, the licence's fee and royalty, the first stone or ore in the store.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Why did you find what you found where you found it? Was the licence's price clear?

## 7. Design and drive a wagon into mud (5 minutes)
Q > Design a vehicle. Load the ox wagon, change the front axle to a pivoting one, look at the stats pane (pull needed on dry road and in mud, cargo, speed). Save it and commission it from a wheelwright or carpenter (or build it yourself part by part: Q > Work on it).
- When it is built, hitch two oxen, load it heavily (about 900 kg), and drive it (E beside it) onto a dirt road after rain or into a muddy field.
- When it sticks: unload some of the cargo (\ drops 100 kg) and push, or dig ([); then reload on firm ground and drive to the market.
- Look for: the wagon slowing as the ground softens, the stuck message, wheels wearing (Q > Inspect), the stats pane's mud figure matching what happened.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Why did it stick there and not earlier? Did the pane's "needs N oxen in mud" warn you?

## 8. Make a will (2 minutes)
Beside a priest or the reeve, Q > Make a will. Leave your business to one person and the rest by the law; it costs 2 crowns.
- The will panel's right column is the estate as it stands today: who gets what if you die now. Look at it before and after you set the bequests.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Was it clear what the law would do without the will?

## 9. The tanner's widow's first hour (5 minutes)
Start a new game as the tanner's widow (the eighth origin). Play her first hour at 1x without dev commands.
- Find that the tannery is hers, on a lease with her husband's arrears; pay them at the deeds screen (or decide not to); meet the journeyman; take the first job he suggests.
- Then let a game week pass at 8x with the journeyman working and check the Ledger.
- Look for: what the opening tells you about the tannery, the arrears and the lord; the first job paying; the tannery's first season row.
- **Did you understand why that happened?** Did you know what you owed and to whom? Did "keep the tannery" feel like a choice?

## After the session
- Which item felt most worth the time, and which felt slowest? (Building's pace is the plan's first risk: "did building feel worth it?")
- Was anything a surprise you could not explain from what the game showed? Note it with F8.
- Save and quit; reload the last save and check one thing you built, one business and the wagon are as you left them.
