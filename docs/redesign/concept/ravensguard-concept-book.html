look & play concept

Falling LeavesRavensguard

A walkable medieval valley where everyone has a body, a home and a price. You start with nothing. You can end with a banner.

the game in one breath

You are one person in a valley that keeps going without you

Walk it, work it, fight in it, buy it. Nothing glows on the map unless you pin it there. Your ambition tells you what you want, and the world shows you how people get it. It runs in real time with pause, and three clocks tick at once.

seconds

With your own hands

Walk the street, talk, haggle, steal, fight. Space pauses everything. Every action is a click or a key, and the key is always printed on the button.

hours and days

Jobs and journeys

Guard a caravan to Ashby, haul hides for 2 crowns a day, pay your squad on Sunday. Food runs down. Your workers keep their shifts while you are away.

weeks and seasons

Wars and fortunes

Harvests set bread prices. Lords raise levies from their villages and march. Your smithy turns a profit or goes idle. Your ambition moves forward or slips away.

  1. Party bar. Crowns, food in days rather than units, and carry weight. Your companions' health is a coloured dot; hover one to see the wound.
  2. Time. Pause, 1×, 2×, 4×, and 8× when nothing hostile is in sight. A game hour passes in 3 real minutes at 1×. The minimap is the painted world map, zoomed in.
  3. Ambition. Your own goal, with steps. The hint says where people find that kind of work. Pin it to the map if you want a marker; nothing is pinned by default.
  4. Right-click anyone. Every person has the same menu: talk, trade, hire, recruit, rob, attack. Before you commit a crime, it shows whether you are seen.
  5. Roofs lift when your people go inside, so you can see what they are doing.
  6. Property is physical. Staked lots with signs. You buy this one from the reeve, then build on it yourself.
  7. The log explains itself. Anything surprising has a "why" link that opens the cause in the chronicle.
  8. Screens unlock as your life grows. Build and Ledger open with your first deed or claim banner, Faction when you serve a lord.

How this moment plays

You click Hedda, or walk up and press E. She has hides to sell and a job hauling them to the tannery for 2 crowns a day. You could pick her purse, but three people are watching, and the menu tells you so. Your goal is the staked lot across the street. It costs 450 crowns and you have 191.

What makes it a game

The town is not a menu. Shops are people with stock that comes from the real economy, so prices move for reasons you can trace. When Hedda finishes work at 19:00 she walks home to no. 4, and you can follow her, hire her, or rob her house.

  1. Strength and morale for both sides. Battles end when a side breaks, not when the last man dies.
  2. Pause to aim. T freezes the field while you choose. Without pausing, the game slows down by itself on crits, kills and when your hero is hit.
  3. You fight in the line as your own character. WASD to move, click to attack.
  4. Called shots on a paper-doll. Armour shows as hatching, wounds in red. The odds come from the real armour and the real gap.
  5. Formation orders while you fight: F1–F4 picks a formation, the wheel on R gives orders. Captains run whatever you don't touch.
  6. Nothing is cleaned up. Bodies, severed limbs, blood and spent arrows stay on the ground after the battle and into the next week.
  7. A log you can read, filtered to what happened to you, your allies, or everyone.

How this moment plays

Vell's host caught your levies at the ford. You hold the centre with your retinue and send the horse round their left. A man-at-arms reaches you; you press T, see the gap in his mail at the armpit, and thrust. The callout tells you what happened in plain words.

What happens after

No HP bar resets. Ceolric limps for a week. The men you levied from Hollin are not harvesting, so Hollin's grain falls short this autumn. Prisoners can be ransomed or recruited. The chronicle writes it down: "Day 31: Aldric broke House Vell's left at Cold Ford."

How this moment plays

You lay out the walls, drop an anvil, and hire two labourers from the street. They haul stone while you buy iron in Ashby. When the forge is in, you hire a smith, choose what he makes and what you charge, and leave. The smithy keeps working while you're gone, using the same recipe numbers.

Where it can go

The same tools build a homestead in a wild clearing, a palisade around it, and one day the walls of your own town. Walls you build are real in sieges: a ram breaks the segment it hits, and the breach stays until you repair it.

how it plays

Your first hour or so

One possible start, about 75 real minutes across two game days, playing mostly at 1× and 4× and sleeping at 8×. Nothing here is scripted except where you begin and the ambition you picked.

  1. 0:00

    Day 1, 08:00 · One screen, then you're in the world

    Pick a name and an origin: disgraced squire, tanner's widow, or deserter. Each comes with an ambition. Tick Ironman if you want one life. Under a minute later you are on the road into Oakford with 45 crowns.

  2. 0:05

    Day 1, 09:40 · Oakford doesn't care that you arrived

    People go to work. The notice board at the reeve's house lists a lot on Tanner's Row for 450 crowns. Your ambition says own a smithy, so now you know what the money is for.

  3. 0:20

    Day 1, afternoon · Earn it somehow

    Hauling hides for Hedda pays 2 crowns a day: safe, and far too slow for a 450-crown lot. You haul until noon and make 1. A caravan master at the gate pays 40 to guard tomorrow's run to Ashby, which is fast and dangerous. You sign on, and hire Brann, a deserter drinking in the tavern, for 20 a week.

  4. 0:40

    Day 2, 09:00 · The ford

    Six bandits at a river crossing 4 km out. The game slows when you are hit. You aim at an unarmoured thigh and the man goes down. Brann is knocked out; you drag him onto the cart and the caravan gets through.

    Day 2: Bandits ambushed the Ashby caravan at the ford. The cart survived because Aldric held the crossing.

  5. 1:00

    Day 2, evening · Paid and patched

    You collect your 40 and sell the bandits' gear for 130. A surgeon splints your arm, and the paper-doll shows it: swings are 30% weaker for six days. Iron is dear in Ashby, and its "why" link says the Coldridge mine flooded. You spend 26 on nails to sell in Oakford.

  6. 1:15

    Day 3, morning · A real choice

    You have 190 crowns. Rent a market stall now and start earning, or take a bigger contract that risks Brann's life. In the Ashby tavern you overhear that House Vell is drafting levies two valleys over. Nobody told you. You heard it.

the long game

From wanderer to banner

These are not levels you grind. You reach each one by doing something in the world, like signing a deed or swearing fealty, and you can fall back down. New screens appear when they become relevant.

brutal, but never random

Every loss has a reason you can read

The world is harsh by design. The rule that keeps it fair: the game never hides why something happened. Click any "why" and the chronicle shows the chain of causes. When nothing specific caused a change, it says so: market drift.

Bread in Oakford costs three times its usual price.
  1. The mill at Grimsby burned (day 9).
  2. The Ashby grain caravan was raided at the ford (day 11).
  3. Oakford's granary is down to 4 days.

  1. Two people saw you take a purse in the square (day 14).

  3. Pay it at the reeve's, or stay out of town.
You lost the fight at Cold Ford.
  1. Your left leg was crushed: movement −60%.
  2. Brann fell, and your retinue's morale broke.
  3. The encounter readout put the odds at 1 to 3 before you charged.
