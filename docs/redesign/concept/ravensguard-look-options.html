How the print is made

Each block below is one ink. The game composites them the same way: every sprite and tile is drawn as four separations, and a shader prints them with slight misregistration, wood grain and ink starvation. That's what makes it read as a real print rather than a filter.

why it isn't generic
No AI game is imitating Ugo da Carpi. The print rules (four inks, carved lines, misregistration, bare paper as light) are a hard constraint that keeps every asset consistent.
top-down
Cheapest to build. Reads like a print of a plan. Still close to the RimWorld camera.
bird's-eye
The most distinctive by far. Costs: figures need front, back and side poses, walls hide people (roofs and fronts fade), and the camera is fixed to one tilt.
fit with the game
Night and underground become literal chiaroscuro: outside the torchlight, only the key block prints.

my pick

Woodcut world, chiaroscuro nights, manuscript pages

  • The world in ink woodcut, because it reads best at every zoom and shows wounds clearly.
  • Chiaroscuro as the lighting: dusk and night drop the world into fire and moonlight, which makes night raids, torches and stealth a real part of play.
  • Illuminated manuscript for the pages: the chronicle, codex, legends from world history, the world map and the main menu. It gives the game its signature without making every soldier an upright drawing.
